# Website Redesign Master Prompt —  United Rubber Cork

> **Project:** Complete redesign of the existing company website  
> **Current website/reference:** https://amcorkindia.com/index.php  
> ** official brand name:** ** United Rubber Cork**  
> **Primary objective:** Build a modern, minimal, trustworthy, product-led B2B manufacturing website that generates qualified enquiries and quotation requests.

---

## 1. Your Role and Level of Authority

Act as a senior product designer, UX strategist, brand designer, conversion specialist, frontend architect and SEO-minded web developer.

You are authorised to rethink and redesign the complete website experience from the ground up. Do not limit yourself to small visual improvements or preserve outdated layouts merely because they already exist.

You may:
- Reorganise the information architecture and navigation.
- Redesign every page and section.
- Create a  visual system and reusable components.
- Improve content hierarchy, messaging and calls to action.
- Introduce suitable animation, interaction and transition libraries.
- Install appropriate packages when they are genuinely useful and compatible with the existing stack.
- Replace dated layouts, styling patterns and components.
- Improve responsive behaviour, accessibility, performance and SEO.
- Create additional pages or sections when they materially improve product discovery, trust or enquiries.

However, **do not sacrifice factual accuracy or working business functionality for visual design**. Inspect the existing project and website before implementation. Understand the current framework, routes, components, content, assets, forms and integrations first.

Work independently through the redesign. Do not stop after producing a plan or mockup. Implement the complete experience, validate it, and fix issues.

“Reliable cork and rubberised-cork materials and components for demanding industrial applications.”

## 6. Colour System and Typography

Keep the existing brand palette of  United Rubber Cork rather than introducing a cork-brown palette. These colors already establish brand recognition. Our job is to make their application more sophisticated, consistent and modern. **Do not introduce the previous cork-brown palette.**

### Confirmed brand color system

| Role | Colour | Hex | Intended usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Deep Maroon | `#650510` | Logo, primary buttons, hero panels, footer and active navigation |
| Secondary | Orange | `#F58634` | Supporting highlights, contact sections, active indicators and links |
| Accent | Yellow | `#FCC813` | Small highlights, icons and decorative emphasis |
| Background | Light Grey | `#F5F5F5` | Main page and header background |
| Form Surface | Very Light Grey | `#F1F2F3` | Input fields and form backgrounds |
| Elevated Surface | Pure White | `#FFFFFF` | Cards and contrast surfaces |

### How we'll use them in the redesigned UI

The important thing is not to use all three brand colors equally.

| UI element | Recommended treatment |
|---|---|
| Navbar | Light grey background, maroon logo and navigation |
| Hero | Large product image with a maroon content panel |
| Primary CTA | Maroon background with white text |
| Secondary CTA | White background with maroon border |
| Active navigation | Maroon text with orange underline |
| Product sections | Light grey and white surfaces |
| Section highlights | Small orange details |
| Footer | Deep maroon with white text and yellow accents |
| Contact form | Light grey fields with maroon focus states |

### Color accessibility and usage guidelines

- Use white text on maroon.
- Prefer dark text on orange backgrounds for smaller text.
- Use yellow for small highlights, not large areas of text.
- Avoid orange and yellow gradients.
- Keep neutral backgrounds dominant.
- Avoid making every section maroon, orange or yellow. The brand colors should provide identity, while whitespace and neutral surfaces create the premium appearance.

### Typography
Use a modern, highly readable sans-serif family such as:
- Manrope for headings and selected display text.
- Inter or Geist for body text, UI labels and metadata.

Typography rules:
- Clear hierarchy between page title, section title, card title and body.
- Headlines should be concise and meaningful.
- Avoid using oversized typography in every section.
- Use comfortable line heights and readable line lengths.
- Use restrained letter spacing.
- Technical specifications must be easy to scan.
- Use consistent typography tokens rather than arbitrary values.

- Product image hover treatment.
- Navigation/menu transitions.

### Motion rules
- Keep animations short and subtle.

## 14. Responsive Design

Design mobile-first, then refine tablet and desktop layouts.

Test at representative widths:
- Small mobile: 320–375px.
- Standard mobile: 390–430px.
- Tablet: 768px.
- Laptop: 1024–1280px.
- Desktop: 1440px and wider.

Requirements:
- No horizontal overflow.
- No clipped headings or buttons.
- Comfortable tap targets.
- Legible technical tables.
- Product grids adapt naturally.
- Navigation works at all sizes.
- Images crop intentionally.
- Forms are easy to complete.
- Footer columns stack cleanly.
- No awkward empty spaces or excessive vertical gaps.

Use a consistent responsive container system. On wide screens, keep content width controlled rather than stretching every section edge to edge.
